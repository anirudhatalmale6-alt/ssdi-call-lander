What happens when you call

Calling the number on this page connects you with an intake representative. That representative asks preliminary questions and may refer your information to a participating law firm or non-attorney representative who handles disability claims. Whether any firm or representative accepts your matter is entirely their decision, and no one is obliged to take it.

How we are paid

We are paid by law firms and representatives for advertising and for referring the information callers provide. That means we have a financial interest in you calling. We do not charge you anything for the call, and what we are paid does not change depending on the outcome of your claim.

Fees for representation

Fees charged by a representative in a Social Security Disability matter are regulated by the Social Security Administration and normally must be approved by it. In most cases a representative is paid only if benefits are awarded, out of past-due benefits, subject to the limits the SSA sets. Any representative who takes your matter is required to explain their fee agreement to you in writing before you agree to it.

No promises about outcome

We do not promise that you will qualify for benefits, that a claim will be accepted, or that any particular amount will be awarded. Most first applications for disability benefits are denied. Prior results do not guarantee a similar outcome, and no statement on this site should be read as a prediction about your situation.

No relationship is created by calling

Contacting us does not create an attorney-client relationship or a representative relationship. That relationship begins only when you and a firm or representative sign a written agreement.

Availability

Services described here are not available in every state, and not every caller can be helped. Some matters cannot be accepted for reasons that have nothing to do with the strength of the claim.
